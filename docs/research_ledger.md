## Where the evidence changed the build prompt

| # | Prompt said | Evidence says | Change made |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Paired significance "bootstrap or Wilcoxon" | Wilcoxon and sign tests have inflated Type I error for mean-effectiveness hypotheses at RecSys sample sizes (Urbano et al., SIGIR 2019; Ihemelandu & Ekstrand, SIGIR 2023). Paired t and randomisation tests hold nominal α. | Primary test is a **paired t-test** on per-user means; a **sign-flip permutation test** is a robustness check; a **paired bootstrap 95% CI** and **d_z** are always reported. Wilcoxon is not used. ([03 §B3](research_notes/03_nbr_evaluation.md)) |
| D2 | Holm correction for multiple comparisons | Holm controls FWER (right for "does Mitate beat X" claims). For exploratory many-model leaderboards Benjamini–Yekutieli is more powerful and still valid under dependence (Ihemelandu 2024). | **Holm** for confirmatory Mitate-vs-baseline families, **BY** for full leaderboards. |
| D3 | Global temporal split, targets unspecified | Time to Split (RecSys 2025): GTS-*Successive* is the reference; *Random* agrees best (τ=0.91); *First* and *All* targets disagree (τ≤0.70) and are not recommended. Global-temporal validation with the *Last* target aligns best with test. | Test = **Successive** targets averaged within user; validation = **Last** target before T_val; retrain on train+val. |
| D4 | NDCG@k | The NBR reality-check tables use IDCG over all \|T\| (code), not min(k,\|T\|) (their Eq. 2). | Standard NDCG (min(k,\|T\|)) is the protocol metric; `ndcg_li2023` exists only to reproduce published NBR numbers. |
| D5 | "Amazon `bought_together` as complementary ground truth" | The field is **null for 100% of items** in all 34 Amazon 2023 metadata files (GitHub issue #48; re-checked: 0/603,274 grocery items). | Complement ground truth = **Amazon 2018 `also_buy`** (29% of grocery items populated) + held-out co-purchase pairs. |
| D6 | GRU4Rec (official) as a baseline | Official code license: "Non-commercial use only". Third-party ports are broken (Hidasi & Czapp, RecSys 2023). | Kept as a **benchmark-only** baseline fetched at run time; never vendored, never shipped. |
| D7 | BPR-MF baseline | implicit's BPR performs poorly in the RecSys 2024 BPR replicability study. | BPR kept for completeness (prompt requires it) but flagged; the iALS baseline follows Rendle et al. (RecSys 2022) tuning. |
| D8 | SASRec-CE/SS, gSASRec, eSASRec | Pairwise-transition probes (PCTM, SeqRules) beat faithful eSASRec by 15–38% on Amazon-scale data and lose only at ML-20M scale (Petrov et al., RecSys 2026, arXiv 2608.19833). No study measures the transformer crossover with data size. | PCTM + SeqRules are **first-class sources**; SASRec variants are benchmarked and enter the blend only when they win on validation. This crossover is one of the benchmark's open questions. |
| D9 | "an LLM-based cold-start method" (e.g. small LoRA LLM) | The PMC small-LLM paper's notion of cold start does not match ours and gives no CPU cost data. LLM text embeddings are the credible, affordable form. | The LLM cold-start baseline is **LLM text embeddings of item text** (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B), with LLM-written item profiles as an ablation and small-LLM zero-shot reranking as a negative control. |
| D10 | Synerise RecSys 2025 dataset | CC BY-NC 4.0, and the download returns 404. | Excluded. |
| D11 | Instacart for grocery | Kaggle competition page and original S3 both fail (404/403); no absolute timestamps, so no global temporal split. | Instacart used **only** for pipeline validation (reality-check reproduction). Grocery benchmark = **Dunnhumby The Complete Journey** (real day indices), pending the user accepting its terms. |
| D12 | SANSA for large catalogs | Needs scikit-sparse → SuiteSparse CHOLMOD with GPL-2.0+ modules. Needed only above ~30–50K items. | Dense EASE up to 25K items (all simulated stores fit). SANSA is benchmark-only above that. |
| D13 | Interleaving for "less traffic than an A/B test" | The 10–100× sensitivity figures come only from large platforms (Amazon, Airbnb, Bing). Nothing shows they transfer to small stores. | Interleaving ships, but its sensitivity for small stores is measured in our own simulations and stated as unverified for real small-store traffic. |
| D14 | DPDP "notified November 2025" | Rules G.S.R. 846(E) dated 13 Nov 2025. Substantive duties start ~13 May 2027. s.9(3) bans tracking/behavioural monitoring of anyone under 18, with no e-commerce exemption. | Mitate has a **no-profile path** for minors and for shoppers without consent. ([privacy.md](privacy.md)) |
| D15 | OTTO test = the official test week | The official test sessions are **truncated at a random point** (the rest are the competition's hidden labels): 13.9M events in that week against ~54M in an average train week. A 1K-interaction store built on it had 6 test events. | Only the official train file is used: `t_test` = its last 7 days, `t_val` = the 7 days before (2022-08-14 / 08-21 / 08-28, 22:00 UTC). Found and fixed before any result of record; store caches and the co-browse graph are keyed by `t_test`. |
| D16 | gSASRec with its published defaults (k = 256 negatives, t = 0.75) | On a 756-item OTTO store, gBCE/BCE with k ≥ 4 negatives plateaus at popularity level (val NDCG@10 ≈ 0.035) on CPU and MPS for every t tried, while k = 1 reaches 0.172 and full-softmax SASRec 0.186. The loss was checked line by line against `asash/gSASRec-pytorch` (same β, same per-position mean over 1 + k entries) and learns a synthetic chain. Also tried without effect: untied output embeddings (the reference default) and the reference's N(0, 1) output initialisation. Small catalogs make k = 256 a third of all items. Cause not explained. | `negatives` ∈ {1, 4, 16, 64, 256} and `t` ∈ [0, 1] are tuned for gSASRec inside the same 30-trial budget. Reported as "gSASRec (tuned k, t)"; the collapse is listed as an open issue in results.md. |
| D17 | Amazon Reviews 2023 as a size-crossover dataset ("general e-commerce with text") | Measured on simulated Grocery stores (2018+ reviews, global split): at 10K interactions only 15 of 311 test shoppers have any prior purchase in the store (127 of 2,575 at 100K). Reviewers rarely review twice in one category, so a next-basket benchmark would be ~95% cold-start users and say nothing about how methods scale with data. | Amazon Grocery is **not** in the size-crossover benchmark. It is used for what only it offers: (1) the day-one, catalog-only stage (text embeddings vs popularity for shoppers with no history) and (2) the complements placement, scored against Amazon 2018 `also_buy`. The crossover datasets remain OTTO, H&M and Dunnhumby. |
| D18 | Every model tuned with 30 trials on each of the 5 stores at every size, 1K–1M | Measured on this machine (M1, 8 GB): non-neural models tune in seconds at every size, but each SASRec-family trial costs 11–15 s per epoch at 100K on MPS, capped at 900 s per trial. The full grid is ≈ 14 h at 30K, 50 h at 100K, 150 h at 300K and 500 h at 1M. | Fixed before any store above 10K was evaluated (`tuning.scale` in the benchmark config). **≤ 10K:** unchanged. **≥ 30K:** every model gets 20 trials (still equal across models) on the bucket's first store; the other four stores reuse that configuration and re-select only the epoch count on their own validation window, so each store keeps its own validation score for the "validation-selected single method" comparison. **≥ 100K:** additionally, gSASRec, eSASRec and GRU4Rec are not run; SASRec-CE, named as the blend's neural source before any result existed, is. Results tables mark models not run. Neural models at 300K–1M may hit the 900 s trial cap; epochs reached are reported. |
| D19 | "Goes well with" from co-purchase and the merchant-approved category graph only | On a development Amazon Grocery store (seed 0, never reported), Mitate's complements rail filled 1% of its slots even at 100K interactions: review data rarely puts 5 shoppers behind a pair. Raw `also_buy` turned out to be dominated by same-kind items (same-category popularity scored NDCG@10 0.35 at 1K), so it rewards substitutes, which is the wrong goal for this rail. On the cross-category part of `also_buy`, text similarity restricted to other categories was the best method at 1K, 10K and 100K (0.29 / 0.22 / 0.21). | (1) Complements are also scored against `also_buy_cross` (pairs in different leaf categories), defined by rule and reported next to raw `also_buy`. (2) A third complement tier fills empty slots with the most text-similar products from other categories, always below co-purchase and approved pairings, with no reason attached. (3) Placement results are reported on fresh store seeds 1–5; seed 0 is the development store on which these decisions were made, after its numbers were seen. |
| D20 | "Goes well with" tier 1 keeps every co-bought item, whatever its category | The synthetic demo store (not a benchmark input) showed the rail for a skillet led by another frying pan, and the rail for a spatula filled with spatulas: shoppers do co-buy substitutes, so the counted reason was true, but the rail duplicated "Similar items". This was noticed **after** the D19 results on reported seeds 1–5 had been seen (`experiments/results/placements_d19/`), so the change is post hoc with respect to those seeds. | Tier 1 now excludes items in any anchor's own category; the other tiers already did. The placement benchmark is rerun on the same seeds and both runs are reported: D19 (before) and D20 (after), with the change and its timing stated next to the numbers. No other setting was changed. |
| D21 | Every bucket runs on this machine (Apple M1, 8 GB) | Stores of 300K–1M events would swap heavily in 8 GB, and the neural grid at ≥ 30K is days of compute here (D18). At these sizes the 900 s neural trial cap binds, so the hardware changes how many epochs a model gets, and therefore its score; at ≤ 10K it does not (SASRec trials average about 75 s at 10K). | Decided before any store above 10K was evaluated. Buckets ≤ 10K stay on the M1 (superseded by D23). Buckets ≥ 30K run on Kaggle (GPU T4 x2, 30 GB RAM) through `scripts/kaggle/mitate_bench.ipynb`, with every model in a bucket on the same accelerator. The only configuration change is `tuning.neural_device: cuda`. Each run records its hardware in `experiments/results/otto/_hardware/`, and before running it must reproduce a known store bit for bit (`bench.fingerprint`), so local and Kaggle sample identical stores from identical input. Results tables state the hardware per bucket; efficiency numbers are only compared within one machine. |
| D22 | H&M runs like the other datasets | The competition rules allow non-commercial research use and forbid redistribution. `transactions_train.csv` holds 31.8M rows from 1.37M customers, beyond what this machine can ingest in 8 GB alongside other work. | Decided before any H&M store was sampled. Every H&M bucket, 1K–1M, runs on Kaggle (GPU T4 x2) through the same notebook with `DATASET = "hm"`, and `bench.data.hm` ingests the attached competition files there. Only results come back: scores, tuned settings and top-20 lists in store-local ids. Sampled stores and every H&M id stay on Kaggle. Since H&M is never sampled on another machine there is no cross-machine fingerprint, and each run still records its hardware. Task: next basket, one basket per customer-day; test is the last 7 days of the training file, validation the 7 before; GRU4Rec is not run, because a session here is a one-day basket. |
| D23 | Buckets ≤ 10K stay on the M1 (D21) | Local runs stopped at the user's request with 10K incomplete: SASRec-family models finished only on the seed-0 store, and GRU4Rec on no store at any size. Tuning records keep each model's total time but not each trial's, so they cannot show that no 10K trial reached the 900 s cap (SASRec averaged 75 s a trial there). Finishing on Kaggle would put one bucket's neural models on two accelerators. | Decided before any Kaggle result at ≤ 10K existed. All OTTO buckets 1K–10K rerun from scratch on Kaggle (GPU T4 x2), so every reported OTTO result comes from one accelerator type. The M1 results already computed are kept under `experiments/results/otto-m1/` and published as a cross-hardware replication: the same stores, the same configuration, a different machine. |
| D24 | Five stores per bucket, seeds 0–4 | The first H&M run on Kaggle stopped at its second 1K store: none of its shoppers ordered in the 7-day validation week. A store of 1K purchase events spread over two years averages about ten events a week, so an empty window is an expected draw, not a fault. Nothing can be scored on such a store, for any method. | Decided after that crash and before any H&M score was read. A bucket's stores are the first five seeds, counting from 0 and continuing into up to ten spare seeds (5–14), whose store can be sampled and has at least one validation and one test query. Every seed passed over is recorded with its reason in `stores.json`. The rule is the same for every dataset and changes no OTTO store, since none has been passed over. Conditioning on at least one query in each window favours no method, but it does exclude the very quietest stores, so results at small H&M sizes describe stores with some purchases in both weeks. |
| D25 | The product serves "the Mitate blend" | Without a trained ranker the engine fell back to rank fusion, and the H&M ablation (stage 2) shows rank fusion losing 0.04–0.21 NDCG@10 to the learned ranker at every store size. A shop never has a ranker trained on its own domain: it gets whatever ships. | Decided before any OTTO stage-2 result was read. The package ships the LambdaMART rankers trained on pooled OTTO stores (CC BY 4.0, the only dataset whose derivatives may ship), and the engine uses them by default; rank fusion remains only for a source combination no shipped ranker covers. No ranker trained on H&M, Dunnhumby or Amazon ever ships. Because the shipped ranker meets other domains in practice, its transfer is measured: H&M test stores are also scored with the OTTO-trained ranker (`mitate_otto_full`) and reported next to H&M's own (`mitate_full`). |
| D26 | Grocery repeat purchase on Dunnhumby, at every store size | The source files carry no license of their own; the site terms allow research and non-commercial use of downloaded material and forbid modifying it. The data holds 2.6M transaction lines from 2,500 households over 711 days, including fuel, coupons, postage, prescriptions and voided lines. On the ingested data, store sampling at 100K events and above finds no sub-catalog with enough pre-test events even after widening the assortment (`bench.simulator.sample_store`), and a 7-day window holds about 2,700 baskets in total, too few for small stores. | Decided after ingesting and sampling one store per size, before any model was fitted or evaluated. Only merchandise is kept: lines with quantity or value at or below zero and thirteen non-merchandise departments are dropped (`bench.data.dunnhumby.NOT_MERCHANDISE`). Test is the last 28 days, validation the 28 before. Stores run at 1K–30K events (`max_bucket` in the configuration). The source CSVs go unmodified to a private Kaggle dataset and are ingested there; as with H&M, only scores leave Kaggle, nothing fitted on Dunnhumby ships, and nothing from it is redistributed. Models as for H&M; the OTTO-trained ranker is also scored (D25). |

## Benchmark protocol

| Component | Choice | Evidence | Alternatives considered | Why it won | Caveats |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Split | Global temporal split; T_val < T_test chosen by minimum target counts; retrain on train+val | Time to Split, Gusak et al. RecSys 2025 ([arXiv 2507.16289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16289)); Meng et al. RecSys 2020; Ji et al. TOIS (leakage) | Leave-one-out; random user split; per-user last basket | Only GTS simulates "deploy at T, serve until retrain". LOO inflated NDCG@10 by 16.5–90% and reshuffled model rankings (Le et al., RecSys 2025, via the thesis version). | Small stores have few test targets; T_test is set by a minimum target count, and the period lengths are reported. |
| Targets | Test: Successive, averaged within user (or anonymous session). Validation: Last. | Time to Split Table 6 | First, All, Random | Successive is the reference; Last validation tracks it best. | Successive weights heavy shoppers equally after per-user averaging, by design. |
| Ranking | Full catalog, no sampled negatives | Krichene & Rendle KDD 2020; Hidasi & Czapp RecSys 2023 | Sampled 100-negative metrics | Sampled metrics do not preserve model order. Small catalogs make full ranking cheap. | — |
| Metrics | NDCG, Recall, MRR, HitRate @ {5,10,20}; Recall/PHR repeat vs explore; coverage, novelty, ILD, ARP, APLT, Gini, entropy | [03 Exact definitions](research_notes/03_nbr_evaluation.md) | — | Standard definitions with cited formulas; repeat/explore split from Li et al. TOIS 2023. | Beyond-accuracy metrics can be gamed in NBR (TREx, SIGIR 2024); always paired with Recall_expl. |
| Statistics | Paired t + sign-flip permutation, bootstrap CI, d_z, Holm / BY | D1, D2 above | Wilcoxon, bootstrap-shift test | Calibrated Type I error at n≥25. | At large n everything is significant: decisions read the CI and d_z. |
| Tuning | Optuna TPE, seeded, sequential, **equal trial budget** for every tunable model, validation NDCG@10; epochs tuned by validation monitoring | Rendle et al. 2019; Shehzad & Jannach RecSys 2023; Dacrema et al. 2019 | Grid search; per-model budgets | Unequal tuning is the most common way baselines "lose". | Equal trials ≠ equal compute; wall-clock is logged per model. |
| Leakage tests | (1) perturbation test: everything after the cutoff replaced by noise must leave predictions bitwise identical; (2) the 15-item checklist in [03 §B5](research_notes/03_nbr_evaluation.md) | Ji et al. TOIS; Hidasi & Czapp 2023; Sun SIGIR 2023 | Manual review | The perturbation test catches leakage through any channel without enumerating channels. | The RecSys 2025 leakage paper itself is closed-access; its list was not read. |

| Complements | Category-level complement graph generated offline by a local LLM (`qwen3:4b`, Apache-2.0), merchant-reviewed, mapped to SKUs via embeddings + attribute filters | P-Companion (Amazon); McAuley et al. | Blend | LightGBM LambdaMART over union candidates; store-volume features; trained on validation labels pooled across store sizes | Qin et al. | ANN | Exact NumPy matmul to ~50K items; USearch above | [04 §7](research_notes/04_coldstart_llm_embeddings_ltr.md) | hnswlib (stale), FAISS | Small catalogs do not need ANN | — |
| Offline LLM | `qwen3:4b` (Apache-2.0); `phi4-mini` (MIT) as a second opinion | [05 §3](research_notes/05_datasets_licenses.md) | Llama 3.x (naming clause), Gemma 3 (flow-down terms), Ministral-8B-2410 (research only) | Fits 8 GB; clean license | Model digest and prompt hash recorded with every generated graph |
